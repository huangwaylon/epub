# コンビニ人間 — *Convenience Store Woman* — glossary

Follows docs/translation.md: Hepburn without macrons, honorifics kept, 「」 → "…", 『』 inside dialogue → '…', …… → "...", no Japanese characters in the English.

## 1. Narrator voice

- Furukura Keiko, first person "I", 36, has worked 18 years at the same store. Her Japanese is plain, exact and literal. She describes people as organisms and parts (skin, membranes, cells, the eyes/nose/mouth "opening all at once") and she does not comment on emotion. Keep that flatness. Do not add warmth, irony markers or explanation, and do not smooth out her odd logic ("Why? It's already dead." / "That's why I've been saying so").
- Tense: follow the Japanese unit by unit. The frame and habitual passages are in present tense (the opening shift, units 0–33; the 8 a.m. routine, 137–193; "I make it a rule to…"), so keep them in the present. Narrated events are in past tense. Do not change tense in the middle of a unit.
- Her speech is copied from others (half Izumi-san, half Sugawara-san, see §2). When the text says she speaks "in Sugawara-san's way", give her line bright, clipped energy. Her own questions are polite, sincere and literal ("Is that something that would be good for me if I did it?").
- 「」 used as scare quotes in narration ("店員", "普通", "お客様", "朝", "声") become double quotes in English, and the word inside is the same fixed term every time.

## 4. Tone notes and pitfalls in the drafts

Spot-checked about 45 drafts against the Japanese. Across all 1,238 drafts, 147 contain 「」 or Japanese characters, and 145 have bare "Shiraha" against 71 with "Shiraha-san". The drafts are unreliable, so retranslate from the `ja` and do not patch the draft.

Systematic errors:
1. **Quote marks.** 「」 are left in the English, sometimes one per sentence. Curly quotes are mixed with straight ones. Single dialogue lines are split into several single-quoted fragments. Examples: 1214 `「I realized it. 「More than a human…`; 1082 `「I heard!」 Good for you!」`; 270 `'Ah, today you are with the new person, Shiraha.' 'He trained…'`.
2. **Honorifics.** -san is dropped, and "Mr."/"Ms." appear in 53 units: 688 "Mr. Shiraha", 684 "Ms. Furukura", 139 "Mr. Dat". Tuan's name is misspelled as "Thuan-kun" (961).
3. **Additions and misalignment.** 184 and 312 append "We chanted." 961 carries a sentence from unit 419 ("There was a queue at the register…"). 706 inserts a stray "Someone said."
4. **Register flattened.** Dialogue has no contractions and is stiffly formal ("It is like a religion." for 宗教みたいっすね at 321). Shiraha's rudeness, the manager's slang, Sugawara's bounce and Tuan's non-native speech (964, rendered as fluent English) are all lost.
5. **Wrong or unfixed terms.** 889 and 893 use "Karaage-kun sticks". 店員 alternates between clerk, employee and worker. 底辺 varies between "bottom-feeders" and "the dregs", which breaks Keiko's count of four uses.
6. **Literalisms and garbles.** 609 "people who are not upright are processed"; 1217 "a convenience store clerk animal" (should be "an animal called a convenience store worker"). There is also tense drift inside a unit (3: "I react… there was a man").

Also: do not soften the sexual and violent language (the shovel, "rape", "erection", "uterus", "mating"). Let the deadpan comedy come through literal wording, never through added commentary.
