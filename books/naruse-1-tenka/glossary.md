## Characters
**成瀬あかり Naruse Akari** (なるせ). Born 14 May 2006. Talks in plain, declarative, slightly masculine-stiff Japanese (〜だ, 〜だろう, 〜ではないか, すまない, 〜してくれ) to everyone, adults included; Nishiura: "like an RPG villager." Deadpan, literal, precise with numbers, safety-first, never nervous. English: formal, clipped, correct; few contractions; no slang; **not archaic** (no "shall," "let us," "thou"). Fixed: おう → "Hey." (hand raised); そうか → "I see."; 構わない → "I don't mind."; それはよかった → "Good."; すまない → "Sorry." Uses Kansai dialect only on stage. Addresses people by bare surname (島崎, 大貫, 西浦, 中橋, 国友); strangers 君 → "you"; a stranger 貴殿 → "sir" ("you, sir"); Inae once 稲枝氏 → "Inae-shi." Called なるぴょん "Narupyon" by karuta teammates; あかりちゃん "Akari-chan" by adults and by Shimazaki as a child.

**島崎みゆき Shimazaki Miyuki**. Same building, born Dec 2006. Standard (Tokyo) speech from Yokohama/out-of-prefecture parents; casual, quick, wry. Narrator of ch1–2: dry, self-deprecating ("an ordinary person"), devoted chronicler of "the History of Naruse Akari." Calls her 成瀬 "Naruse" (bare) in speech and narration. Friends call her みゆみゆ "Miyumiyu"; adults みゆきちゃん "Miyuki-chan".

**稲枝敬太 Inae Keita** (いなえけいた). Born 1977, web designer in Osaka, single, lives with parents. Self-effacing, a little sour. Calls friends Masaru, Takuro (given names). Later a Tokimeki festival committee member (ch6), shy and red-faced.
**吉嶺マサル Yoshimine Masaru**. Lawyer, Tokimeki-zaka office, baby-faced, sociable, organizer; festival committee chair. Calls the girls 成瀬さん/島崎さん.
**タクロー / 笹塚拓郎 Takuro / Sasazuka Takuro**. Boyhood ringleader who moved away abruptly; garbage-truck driver in Osaka. Rough, cheerful.
Classmates: 竜二 Ryuji, 塚本 Tsukamoto, 相澤さん Aizawa-san (Tokyo), 今井さん Imai-san, 安田 Yasuda (Kenya), 田中さん Tanaka-san; teacher 浅井先生 Azai-sensei.

**大貫かえで Onuki Kaede** (おおぬき). Naruse's schoolmate since first grade; anxious, status-watching, sharp-tongued in narration; thinks in "relationship charts" and "lines." Narrates 成瀬 bare; says 成瀬さん aloud to others. Nickname ぬっきー **"Nukki"** (she dislikes it). Naruse calls her 大貫.
**大黒悠子 Oguro Yuko** (Kaede's lunch friend, commutes from Koka; "Yuko," "Kaede"). **須田直也 Suda Naoya** (Kusatsu, glasses, fellow Todai aspirant; "Suda-kun," "Onuki-san"). **高島央介 Takashima Osuke**. **凛華 Rinka**, **鈴奈 Suzuna** (grade-five bullies).

**西浦航一郎 Nishiura Koichiro**. Nishikigi High, Hiroshima; 186 cm, ex-judo; shy, earnest, smitten. Calls her 成瀬さん "Naruse-san." Nickname にっしゃん "Nisshan."
**中橋結希人 Nakahashi Yukito**. His serial-crush friend. Seniors 桃谷先輩 Momotani-senpai, 尾上先輩 Onoe-senpai.

Minor: Shimazaki's mother (Yokohama, blunt, M-1 fan: "Akari-chan's hilarious"); Naruse's mother (quiet, smiling); Naruse's grandmother (Hikone; "Akari's on again today"); 遥香 Haruka, 瑞音 Mizune (badminton club); 川崎くん Kawasaki-kun; 杉本くん Sugimoto-kun; 国友梨良 Kunitomo Rira (pianist); 津島くん Tsushima-kun; 大沢くん Osawa-kun; the liquor-shop lady (酒屋のおばちゃん).

## Tone, voice, pitfalls
The comedy is deadpan: absurd plans stated flatly, noticed by a dry observer, with warmth underneath (grandmother's death in ch1, Takuro in ch3, the parting in ch6). Don't add exclamation, explanation, or jokes; keep understatement ("Naruse is always strange"). Each narrator has a distinct register: Shimazaki quick and ironic; Keita middle-aged and rueful; Kaede prickly and anxious; Nishiura bashful and romantic; ch6 third-person with Naruse's literalism leaking into the narration (exact seconds, measured detergent).

Draft spot-check (40+ units across the trilogy) — systematic errors:
1. **Misalignment**: a unit's draft belongs to a neighbouring unit (frequent in books 2–3). Translate from `ja` only.
2. **Untranslated/garbage**: Japanese copied as draft (ch3 #222 「ぜひ頼むよ！」, #302).
3. **Punctuation**: dialogue with no quotes (ch4–6 throughout), single quotes for dialogue (ch2), 「」 left in, curly ’ “.
4. **Names**: Western order ("Kaede Onuki," "Akari Naruse"); ぬっきー as "Nuki"; 稲枝氏 → "Mr. Inae" (use "Inae-shi").
5. **Naruse's voice inflated to archaic**: まずは三階に上がろう → "First, let us proceed to the third floor"; 会えなくなるわけではないし… → "It is not as if we shall never see each other again…" Use plain stiff English: "First, let's go up to the third floor." Also meaning slips: コーンフレークらしきもの → "something that sounds like cornflakes" (it is "something that seems to be cornflakes"); padding such as "What about you, Keita? What are you doing?"
