# 森崎書店の日々 — translator's glossary

Follows docs/translation.md: Hepburn without macrons, Japanese name order, honorifics kept, 「」→ "…", 『』→ '…' inside dialogue, titles plain in narration, ……→ "...", ——→ "—", no Japanese characters in English.

## 1. Parts

| Part | Title JA → EN | Narrator / tense | Premise |
|---|---|---|---|
| part0001 (0–718) | 森崎書店の日々 → *Days at the Morisaki Bookshop* | Takako, first person "I", past tense. 帰還 in the title is formal, almost military. It pays off in part0003 770, 「森崎桃子、ただいま帰還しました」 with a salute. Render that line as "Morisaki Momoko, reporting back!" so it echoes "Return".

Family terms: 母 → my mother; 姉さん (Satoru, of Takako's mother) → my sister; 祖父 → my grandfather (Satoru's 親父 → my dad/my old man); 祖母 → my grandmother; ひいおじいちゃん → my great-grandfather (founder).

Forms of address, fixed:
- Narration 叔父 / 叔父さん → "my uncle". Narration サトル叔父(さん) → "Uncle Satoru".
- Vocative 叔父さん → "Uncle". Vocative サトル叔父さん → "Uncle Satoru".
- 桃子おばさん → "Aunt Momoko". 桃子さん → "Momoko-san", in both narration and speech.
- Satoru's あいつ (for Momoko) → "she", or "Momoko" where needed. Never "that guy".
- 睡眠魔人 → "the Sleep Monster".
- ひょうろく玉 (Sabu and Takako, of Satoru) → "goofball".

## 4. Tone notes and pitfalls

Tone: gentle and comic, with warmth that is never syrupy. Takako undercuts herself with deadpan asides ("Who on earth could be so mean? Me, of course."). Her similes are deliberately absurd; keep them without explaining. Examples: the monk's bell ringing in her head (22, 26, recurring), tears as oil, a frog falling from the sky, the sun glaring like a teenage boy. Satoru's and Momoko's speeches are earnest; do not ironize them. Keep repeated words repeated. 〝そこそこ〟 → "so-so" four times. ずるい → "unfair" throughout part0003 (620–648). 逃げるな (596) → "Don't run away"; Takako quotes it back at 665, so the English must match. Render onomatopoeia as verbs, not transliterations. Japanese present-tense narration (historical present) → English past.

Drafts exist only for part0001. 185 of 719 are empty; part0003 has none. About 40 were spot-checked. Use drafts only as a starting point.

1. **Truncation (most common).** The draft stops after the first sentence or two, and later dialogue, including the key quotes, is dropped.
   - 44 loses 「じゃあ、また」.
   - 73 loses 「貴子ちゃんなら大歓迎さ」.
   - 99 loses the schoolgirl-uniform joke.
   - 327 ends before the Santoka line.
   - 542 drops the boat quote.
   - 504 drops 「君は僕にとっての天使だ」.
   - Also 130, 171, 235, 575, 591, 656.
2. **Dropped openings or dialogue, leaving only narration.**
   - 113 omits 「六千冊！」.
   - 434 omits 「いま、かけてるよ……」, the punchline.
   - 17 omits んん？.
   - 514 drops its first sentence.
   - 517 drops 頑強な自分の檻の中に立てこもるのはやめにしよう.
   - 532 drops its opening question.
3. **Mistranslation and tense errors.**
   - 504 「親戚のおっちゃん」 → "some relative of an uncle" (should be "just some uncle you barely know").
   - 697 「とても心配なんだ」 → "I was very worried" (it is present).
   - 109 しっとり → "moist" (Satoru means "mellow, like a morning after rain").
   - 64 "Hit suddenly with the core of the issue" is too literal.
   - Narration slips into present: 26, 136, 546.
4. **Additions.**
   - 718 adds "never wavering".
   - 136 adds "again".
5. **Style violations.**
   - Western name order: "Saisei Muro", "Kafu Nagai", "Osamu Tezuka".
   - "Jinbocho" should be Jimbocho.
   - Titles in double quotes (347, 370).
   - Stray space before punctuation: `"?" .` (10), `"Ah!" .` (145).
   - "I thought. ." (224).
   - Literal `\n` (49).
   - "the master" vs "Master" varies.
   - Voice flattening: Sabu-san's "Listen to me, young lady" (167) is invented. Satoru's soft rambling becomes neutral prose.
